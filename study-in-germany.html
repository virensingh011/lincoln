Study in Germany

Low or no tuition at public universities, strong engineering and an 18-month job-search permit.

Why study in Germany

Low or no tuition at public universities, strong engineering and an 18-month job-search permit.

Low tuition

Most public universities charge no tuition; you pay a semester contribution.

Strong in engineering

Globally respected technical and applied-science programmes.

Job-search permit

Graduates can stay up to 18 months to find work related to their degree.

Heart of Europe

Easy travel across Europe and a large job market.

Germany at a glance

Tuition (indicative)About EUR 0 at most public universities (Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students around EUR 1,500 per semester). Private universities around EUR 5,000 to 20,000+ a year.
Semester contributionAround EUR 150 to 400 per semester, often including a transport pass.
Funds to showBlocked account of EUR 11,904 for a year (EUR 992 a month).
Main intakesWinter (October) and summer (April).
LanguageEnglish-taught programmes need IELTS or TOEFL; German-taught programmes need German proof.
For Indian applicantsAPS certificate is required before the visa; health insurance and a visa fee of about EUR 75 also apply.

Work and stay

Students can usually work 120 full days or 240 half days a year, and student jobs on campus are common. After graduation, an 18-month permit lets you look for work, which can lead to the EU Blue Card.

Step by step: studying in Germany

  1. Choose programme

    Pick a degree and check entry requirements, language level and application deadlines.

  2. Get APS certificate

    Indian applicants must have their documents verified by APS before applying for the visa.

  3. Apply to universities

    Apply directly or through uni-assist, as each university requires.

  4. Open blocked account

    Deposit the required funds and take student health insurance.

  5. Apply for the visa

    Book an appointment at the German mission and submit admission letter, APS, blocked account and insurance.

  6. Arrive and register

    Register your address, enrol at the university and apply for your residence permit.

Common questions

Is study really free?

Tuition is usually free at public universities, but you still pay a semester fee, insurance and living costs.

Do I need German?

Not for many English-taught programmes. Knowing some German helps with daily life and jobs.

What is APS?

An academic verification certificate that Indian applicants need before applying for a German student visa.
